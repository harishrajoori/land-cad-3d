# Common Family-Home Design Rules

**Scope:** recurring requirements for every property option, architecture review and visualization session
**Machine-readable owner brief:** [`home-requirements.json`](home-requirements.json)
**Canonical Vaastu/source research:** [`../knowledge/VAASTU-KNOWLEDGE.md`](../knowledge/VAASTU-KNOWLEDGE.md)
**Latest decisions:** [`../reviews/DECISION-LOG.md`](../reviews/DECISION-LOG.md)

This document prevents repeated long prompts. Apply it automatically whenever creating, reviewing, comparing or visualizing this family's home.

## 1. Authority and precedence

Use this order when rules conflict:

1. Latest dated owner decision.
2. Current owner brief and machine-readable requirements.
3. Surveyed/legal/safety/engineering constraints.
4. Family-selected Vaastu-consultant and ritual guidance.
5. Canonical source synthesis.
6. Proposed architecture.
7. Visual style.

Never treat an old generated plan, image or superseded model as design authority.

## 2. Status vocabulary

Every rule/check must use one of:

- **FIXED:** owner decision; do not change silently.
- **TARGET:** preferred design value; may adjust with explanation.
- **PREFERENCE:** apply where practical; show trade-off.
- **CONSULT:** exact result depends on selected Vaastu/ritual method.
- **LEGAL:** survey/municipal/engineering fact required.
- **PROPOSAL:** not owner-approved.

Never call an AI concept legally approved, structurally safe or finally Vaastu-certified.

## 3. Fixed family requirements

- G+1 house.
- Ground floor: owner and family.
- First floor: brother and family.
- Independent household entrances.
- No shared internal route between homes.
- First floor reachable while ground home is locked.
- Dedicated external stair and gate.
- Ground floor: **two bedrooms plus one home office**.
- Separate daily pooja and Mallanna pooja rooms.
- One car plus two two-wheelers.
- Garden and children's play/open space.
- No dedicated/permanently reserved Patnam area.

## 4. Property-independent room programme

### Ground floor

- North-facing entrance/foyer where the selected property requirement says so.
- Living and dining as connected family spaces, not a hotel lobby.
- Separate kitchen and compact utility.
- Master sleeping room, dressing and attached bathroom.
- Bedroom 2.
- Home office.
- Common bathroom.
- Daily pooja room.
- Mallanna pooja room and preparation access.
- Short common/private circulation only.

### First floor

Detailed programme is flexible. Default proposal:

- Independent 2BHK.
- Living–dining.
- SE kitchen/utility where feasible.
- Common bathroom and master attached bathroom.
- Optional NE pooja/quiet room.
- Useful terrace/open area.
- Structural/wet-area coordination with ground floor.

## 5. Plot and orientation rules

- Read the selected factual file from `properties/` before designing.
- Use surveyed true north when available; mark assumed north as unverified.
- Keep road side, site gate, house-door wall and door facing as separate facts.
- Record all road sides/widths for corner plots.
- Do not mirror one property plan onto another; compass zones remain absolute.
- Four side lengths do not establish an irregular plot; require a survey polygon/bearings.
- Setbacks, coverage, road widening, floors and height are LEGAL hold points until verified.

### Property 1 entrance

- West road and west gates.
- Ground-house main door physically on north wall, facing north.
- Access: west/NW pedestrian gate → east along north-side path → enter south through north-facing door.
- Exactly one intended ground main house door unless the owner approves another.

### Future south-road preference

Investigate an east-facing house main door through a real route. Do not rotate Vaastu zones or copy Property 1.

## 6. Vaastu operational rules

Use the canonical knowledge document for source details and disagreements.

### High-level defaults

- Daily pooja and Mallanna pooja: NE.
- Kitchen: SE; NW is only an explicitly reviewed alternative.
- Master sleeping zone: SW.
- Bedroom 2: W/NW.
- Office: N/NW.
- Centre: comparatively open/light/circulation.
- Stair: W/SW candidate subject to safety and selected consultant.
- Toilets avoid NE and centre.
- North/east generally lighter/open; south/west heavier/shaded.

### Consultant-only decisions

- Exact entrance pada and counting system.
- Exact toilet sub-zones.
- Water/septic/tank positions.
- Deity/worshipper facing.
- Ayam method and measurement convention.
- Detailed stair sub-zone where sources differ.

Do not convert these to PASS without the selected method, measured coordinates and consultant review.

## 7. Pooja and Mallanna rules

- Daily and Mallanna rooms remain separate unless the owner explicitly changes this.
- Neither room is a passage to the other.
- Both should open independently from a common pooja lobby/preparation area where used.
- No bathroom shares a pooja wall or directly opposes a pooja door.
- Avoid a toilet above principal pooja rooms where practicable.
- Provide controlled direct ventilation appropriate for lamps/incense.

Mallanna room must demonstrate:

- localized raised platform/table-like surface;
- four decorated pandal pillars (not building columns);
- prasadam space in front;
- clear space for four people beyond shrine/offerings;
- unobstructed doorway and circulation;
- family/pujari confirmation for image/facing/platform/pillar dimensions.

No permanent Patnam room/pad is part of the programme.

## 8. Architecture quality rules

- Start with area budget, zoning, adjacency and circulation—not rendered furniture.
- Generate at least three materially different options before selecting one.
- Do not declare infeasibility after one failed packing arrangement.
- Do not force all N/NE functions into one horizontal row; zones may extend in depth.
- Do not stretch rooms to consume spare land; return excess to garden/terrace/store/service use or reduce footprint.
- Avoid rigid 3×3 grids and long hotel-style corridors.
- Every private room opens from foyer/living/short common lobby.
- No bedroom, office, pooja, bathroom or kitchen acts as a passage.
- Visitor/office access should not expose the master/private zone.
- Common bathroom should be near Bedroom 2/office but remain common and screened from entry/dining/pooja.
- Master privacy sequence: shared/private lobby → master → dressing → attached bath.
- Kitchen directly serves dining and has safe service access where practical.
- Preserve useful garden/play/open space.

## 10. Door, circulation and furniture logistics

- Main door: 3 ft 6 in minimum concept target; 4 ft preferred.
- Primary approach/circulation: 4 ft preferred; 3 ft 6 in minimum where straight.
- Bedroom/master door: 3 ft minimum; 3 ft 3 in preferred.
- Office/kitchen door: 3 ft minimum.
- Bathroom door: 2 ft 6 in minimum; 2 ft 8 in preferred.
- Pooja door: about 3 ft unless ritual needs wider.
- Provide approximately 5 × 5 ft manoeuvring at the main foyer and genuinely tight critical turns—not at every doorway.
- Avoid corridors over about 15 ft unless daylit, useful and serving multiple rooms.
- Every door/gate/open connection gets a unique ID.
- Use `G` for gates, `D` for doors and `O` for unhinged wide openings.
- Door coordinates must fit shared walls with realistic jambs.

Test delivery/assembly assumptions for:

- modular sofa component approximately 7 × 3 ft;
- queen mattress/bed components;
- wardrobe panels/modules;
- refrigerator approximately 3 × 3 ft.

Distinguish rigid, modular, knock-down and flexible items. Do not claim a rigid object passes a smaller door without tilt/disassembly evidence.

## 11. Common bathroom convenience

- Keep common bath near Bedroom 2/office through one short common lobby.
- Prefer a door-to-door route within about 15 ft and no more than two simple turns.
- Do not make it ensuite to Bedroom 2.
- Avoid direct view from main entrance, dining table or pooja doors.
- Vent directly outside or through a genuine service/ventilation court.

## 12. Kitchen rules

- Kitchen itself—not utility—occupies the approved SE position.
- For east-facing cooking, the person stands west of the hob and looks east; show hob and standing coordinates accordingly.
- Keep sink safely separated, commonly on north/west counter depending geometry.
- Show worktop depth, standing aisle, refrigerator and exhaust.
- Utility remains smaller than kitchen and does not displace it from SE.
- Service exit must not create uncontrolled/duplicate access.

## 13. Parking and stair

- Use actual car envelope, currently about 6.5 × 15.5 ft plus clearance.
- Use two bike envelopes around 2.5 × 7 ft each.
- Show gate clear width, orientation and a real road-to-bay swept path.
- Keep pedestrian, vehicle and stair gates/routes independent.
- Parking must not block any household entrance or external stair.
- External stair can use a compact safe straight/L/U arrangement; safety, landing/headroom and structure govern exact footprint.
- Stair remains independently accessible while ground home is locked.

## 14. Daylight, ventilation and climate

- Every bedroom and office has a useful exterior window.
- Living/dining receives direct exterior light and cross-ventilation; optional court must actually adjoin/serve it.
- Kitchens, toilets and utility vent directly or through real shafts/courts.
- Do not claim a remote OTS ventilates a room without an opening/adjacency.
- Shade west openings with overhangs, fins/jaali, verandah or planting.
- Use the roof/terrace for heat protection, waterproofing, safe drainage and maintainable services.

## 15. G+1 coordination

- Align primary walls/columns across floors where practical.
- Stack kitchens and wet areas.
- Avoid toilet above ground pooja.
- Continue any light court vertically or detail a ventilated/drained skylight.
- Coordinate stair landing, headroom, upper entrance and privacy.
- Keep tank, solar, stair headroom and roof drains non-conflicting.

## 16. Structural and civil rules

- Prefer structural bays roughly 10–14 ft where architecture permits.
- Identify every longer span/open wall for structural-engineer design.
- Do not call an assumed beam a solved structure.
- Integrate columns with walls/furniture; do not place random columns in circulation.
- Distinguish gross/wall-centreline modules from clear internal dimensions.
- Survey, soil/foundation, RCC/member sizes, seismic/wind, electrical/fire, plumbing/drainage and waterproofing remain professional work.

## 17. Default one-pass architecture workflow

When the user asks to design a property and supplies usable dimensions plus road/access information:

1. Read the factual property or parse it using `properties/NEW-PROPERTY-TEMPLATE.md`.
2. Load current owner requirements, decisions, common rules and Vaastu knowledge automatically.
3. Label survey/legal/consultant unknowns; do not block concept design when provisional assumptions are sufficient.
4. Internally generate at least three materially different options.
5. Score every option using Section 18.
6. Independently self-audit the top option using `reviews/DESIGN-CHECKLIST.md`.
7. Correct internal blockers/majors before presenting it.
8. Present **one strongest complete concept** first, with a concise explanation of why the alternatives ranked lower.
9. Include site/footprint, both floors, room/area schedule, access graph, doors/windows, parking/stair, furniture fit, daylight/ventilation, structure/services, roof/elevation intent, Vaastu matrix and hold points.
10. Create a clear north-up dimensioned plan artifact when the user requests a design, but do not create photoreal imagery before owner approval.
11. Revise only when the owner requests a change or a professional hold point invalidates geometry; avoid repeated cosmetic correction loops.
12. After owner design freeze, visualize from approved geometry and prepare professional handoff.

If critical dimensions or road/access information are missing, ask only the minimum necessary question. Otherwise complete the concept in the first response.

## 18. Internal option scoring

Score each candidate out of 100 before selection:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Daily usability, room proportions and furniture | 20 |
| Access, privacy and independent households | 15 |
| Vaastu alignment and explicit consultant holds | 20 |
| Daylight, ventilation and Karimnagar climate response | 15 |
| Structural simplicity and G+1 service stacking | 15 |
| Parking, stair, garden and site movement | 10 |
| Area efficiency, cost and buildability | 5 |

Reject an option regardless of score if it has an inaccessible room, through-bedroom route, pooja/bath conflict, blocked stair/parking access, silent owner-requirement change or impossible geometry.

“Best” means the highest-scoring defensible concept under current inputs—not objective perfection or professional certification.

## 19. AI behavior rules

### GPT-6 Astra

- Acts as concept architect/reviewer, not certifier.
- Reads repository references before work.
- Calculates from geometry rather than trusting generated prose/images.
- Shows trade-offs and does not silently change fixed decisions.
- Creates/reviews architecture before visualization.

### Gemini or image model

- Uses approved dimensioned plan as immutable geometry.
- Uses style board only for materials/lighting/furniture character.
- Does not invent/move rooms, openings, stairs or roads.
- Generates one view at a time.
- Reports geometry it could not preserve.

## 20. Acceptance gate

A design is ready for visualization only when:

- owner programme and orientation are correct;
- room dimensions/areas and wall convention are explicit;
- access graph is connected and private;
- common bath is convenient;
- furniture and parking fit;
- pooja and bathrooms are separated;
- structural spans and G+1 stacking are identified;
- Vaastu hold points are labelled;
- legal/survey assumptions are visible;
- the option has an owner-approved revision ID.
